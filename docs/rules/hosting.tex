% Each document sets its own heading above this. Times are the server's defaults (serve.py options) and the
% client's (client/src/common/main.c). Text the client writes itself, not from the theme, is quoted as is.
% A platform defines \SignalLost (how its screen shows the link is quiet) and may show \atscreen{lobby}.
A \game\ server holds one game at a time: one region, up to \Rule{maxplayers} seats. Every seat with no
\V{player} at the keyboard is played by a bot, so a game never waits long on someone who isn't there.
Times given here are the usual ones; whoever runs the server can change them.

\subsection{The title screen's status}
The title screen dials the server as soon as it appears, and its status line stays up to date while you
type your handle.

\begin{description}[leftmargin=0pt,labelsep=0.5em]
  \item[\code{\Status{lobby}{2}}] No game yet; 2 \Vp{player} wait to play. \key{\KeyReturn} takes a seat in the lobby.
  \item[\code{\Status{jackin}}] A game is on, and a bot holds at least one seat. \key{\KeyReturn} takes over a bot.
  \item[\code{\Status{away}}] A game is on, every seat is a \V{player}'s, and some have dropped. Only they get back in.
  \item[\code{\Status{full}}] \Rule{maxplayers} \Vp{player} are playing. \key{\KeyReturn} does nothing.
  \item[\code{NO CARRIER}] The server didn't answer. The game dials again.
\end{description}
People at the title screen don't hold a seat yet, and nobody waits for them.

\subsection{Handles}
Your handle is your name at the table, up to 8 letters and digits. Only one seat can use it: a handle
already at the table, a bot's included, is refused with \code{THAT HANDLE IS TAKEN}, and you can type another.
Upper and lower case count as the same. The one exception is coming back to your own seat (see
\emph{Rejoining}).

\subsection{Hosting}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Who hosts:} the first \V{player} into an empty lobby. Your prompt line reads
    \code{START WITH HOW MANY SEATS? \Rule{minplayers}-\Rule{maxplayers}}.
  \item \textbf{Starting:} press \key{\Rule{minplayers}}--\key{\Rule{maxplayers}}. The game starts at once; seats
    nobody has joined go to bots, named like \Bot{0} and \Bot{1}. You can't choose fewer seats than there are
    \Vp{player} waiting: with four in the lobby, the server says \code{\Refusal{seats}{4}{\Rule{maxplayers}}}.
  \item \textbf{Who might come:} the seat rows show who has joined, and the log counts the people at the title
    screen, who may be about to: \code{\Say{door}{2}}. It changes as they arrive, join or leave.
  \item \textbf{No hurry alone:} with nobody else seated, take as long as you like.
  \item \textbf{The host clock:} once someone else is waiting, you have 60 seconds to either start the game or
    let the host duty pass to the next \V{player}. Letting it pass is fine: you keep your seat and play when the
    new host starts. The seconds count down at the right of your prompt line, and the others read
    \code{\Say{hostclock}{ZER0COOL}{60}}. When the clock runs out, everyone reads \code{\Say{newhost}{MAMBA}},
    and the new host has a fresh 60 seconds.
  \item \textbf{An absent host,} on autopilot, hands over at once when an active \V{player} joins. A host who
    leaves hands over to the next \V{player} in the lobby.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Joining a lobby}
Type your handle and press \key{\KeyReturn}. You take the next empty seat and appear in everyone's seat rows. If
the host clock is running, the log tells you how long the host has left. You play when the host starts.

\atscreen{lobby}

\subsection{\VC{jackin}: joining a game in progress}
A newcomer to a game in progress takes over the bot seat worth least (cash plus \V{coin}), as it stands: its
\Game{currency}, \V{coin} and \V{kit} are yours now, and your handle replaces the bot's. You read
\code{\Say{takeover}}, and everyone reads \code{\Say{jackin}{ZER0COOL}{\Bot{4}}}. A decision the bot is already
making stays the bot's; you play from the next one.
\begin{itemize}
  \item Two people going for the last bot seat: the first \key{\KeyReturn} wins. The other reads
    \code{TOO LATE: THE LAST SEAT WAS TAKEN} and stays on the title screen.
  \item The server may disable the jack in function for a game among friends. A game in progress then shows
    \code{\Status{full}}.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{The shot clock}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{The shot clock:} each decision has 60 seconds, counted down at the right of the prompt line. At 0
    your seat's bot makes that one decision. The seat stays yours.
  \item \textbf{Autopilot:} after two decisions in a row made by the bot, you are on autopilot. Everyone reads
    \code{\Say{autopilot}{MAMBA}}, and your seat shows \code{?}. You still get every prompt, but on the bot's
    clock, about 10 seconds, so the game doesn't drag.
  \item \textbf{Back from autopilot:} answer one prompt in time. The full clock comes back, and everyone reads
    \code{\Say{returned}{MAMBA}}.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{\VC{logoff}: leaving on purpose}
Any time after joining, \key{Esc} asks \code{\MakeUppercase{\V{logoff}} NOW? Y/N} on the prompt line.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \key{Y} leaves for good. In a game, a bot takes your seat at once, under a bot's name, and plays at bot
    speed: nobody waits on your shot clock. Everyone reads \code{\Say{logoff}{\Bot{5}}{MAMBA}}. Your old seat is now
    an ordinary bot seat, which anyone, you too, can jack in for. In the lobby, your seat is simply freed.
  \item Any other key stays, and the question you were answering comes back.
\end{itemize}
You land on the title screen with your handle filled in.

\subsection{Switching off, or losing the connection}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{In the lobby:} your seat is freed at once. If you hosted, the next \V{player} does.
  \item \textbf{In a game:} your seat is kept for you and shown \code{?}; its bot plays every decision until you
    are back. Your \Game{currency}, \V{coin} and \V{kit} stay with it.
\end{itemize}
A server notices a clean disconnect at once. A computer switched off, or a network that just stops, takes it up to
about a minute; meanwhile the shot clock covers any decision.

\subsection{Rejoining}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{The connection drops:} after 20 seconds with nothing from the server, \SignalLost. At 30 seconds the game hangs up and dials again by itself. Once back, the
    server sends the whole game again, any decision waiting included. No key needed.
  \item \textbf{After a reboot:} type the same handle on the title screen and press \key{\KeyReturn}. You get your
    own seat back, not a bot's, as long as it is shown away. If the server hasn't noticed the drop yet, your
    handle is still in use (\code{THAT HANDLE IS TAKEN}): wait a minute and try again.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Everybody gone}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Nobody connected:} the game pauses; bots don't play on for nobody. The first one back finds it as
    it was.
  \item \textbf{Paused too long:} after 10 minutes with nobody connected, the game is dropped, with no result,
    and the region is an empty lobby again.
  \item \textbf{Everyone on autopilot} for 10 minutes: the game is dropped too. Those still connected land in a
    fresh lobby, the first of them hosting, and read \code{\Say{abandoned}}.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{After the \V{nullroute}}
When the game ends, everyone sees the final \Game{currency} and the top \V{player}. The table is a lobby again,
with the same host (or the next \V{player} still connected), whose prompt asks for the seats of a rematch.
\VCp{player} still connected keep their seats; a newcomer may take any other chair. A \V{player} who rejoins
after the end sees the result.
