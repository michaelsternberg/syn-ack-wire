\chapter{\VC{jackin}}
\game\ runs on an NES with a FujiNet NES cartridge. Pick a \game\ server in the FujiNet
lobby, which loads the game itself, or in FujiNet's CONFIG mount \code{syn-ack-wire-nes.nes} from the host
\code{syn-ack-wire.fly.dev} and boot it. The lobby passes the server you pick through FujiNet's SD card: without
one, CHIBA-GRID-7 is the server you join.

The title dials the server as soon as it appears. Dial in a handle, up to 8 characters: \key{UP} and \key{DOWN}
change the letter the dial is on, \key{LEFT} and \key{RIGHT} move along the handle, \key{B} deletes a letter. Then
press \key{START}. From the FujiNet lobby, the handle starts as your lobby name.

\chapter{The Screen}
\begin{sloppypar}
Everything you need is on one screen. The NES has room for 32 columns of letters where the other machines have
40, so \game\ lays its screen out in 30 of them, inside the edges a TV cuts off: the syndicates by their short
names, long log lines over two rows. Colour does what inverse does elsewhere: the \V{grid} and the seats on green,
cash and every other syndicate's \V{coin} in yellow, and the bottom two lines purple while the decision is yours.
A yellow cursor marks what \key{A} would answer. This is early in a game, and it's your turn: pick an \V{exploit} to run.
\end{sloppypar}

\renewcommand{\secstatus}{THE PAD \quad // \quad A, B, START \quad // \quad SELECT AS A SHIFT}
\chapter{The Pad}
\game\ is played with the pad in port 1. Each question on the prompt line names the buttons it takes; this
chapter has them all in one place. A yellow cursor sits on the answer \key{A} would give, and the d-pad moves it.
The game checks an answer before sending it and says on the prompt line what's wrong, such as \code{NOT ENOUGH NEW YEN}.

\subsection{Any time}
\begin{keytable}
\kr{\key{SELECT}}{Open the instructions, on the title screen or in the middle of a game: press it on its own.}
\kr{\key{SELECT}+\key{A}}{Move hints on or off: the digits on the \V{grid} that show where each \V{exploit} in your \V{kit} can run.}
\kr{\key{SELECT}+\key{B}}{Sound on or off. A chord tells you it's on.}
\end{keytable}

\subsection{The title screen}
\begin{keytable}
\kr{\key{UP}\ \ \key{DOWN}}{Change the letter the dial is on: A--Z, 0--9, then the signs a handle can have.}
\kr{\key{LEFT}\ \ \key{RIGHT}}{Move along the handle; past its end, \key{UP} or \key{DOWN} adds a letter. \key{A} moves on too.}
\kr{\key{B}}{Delete the letter the dial is on.}
\kr{\key{START}}{\VC{jackin}.}
\end{keytable}

\subsection{The instructions}
\begin{keytable}
\kr{\key{RIGHT}}{Next page.}
\kr{\key{LEFT}}{Previous page.}
\kr{\key{A}\ \ \key{B}\ \ \key{START}}{Back to where you were. \key{SELECT} does the same.}
\end{keytable}

\subsection{Hosting}
\begin{keytable}
\kr{\key{UP}\ \ \key{DOWN}}{The number of seats, \Rule{minplayers}--\Rule{maxplayers}.}
\kr{\key{A}}{Start the game with that many. Bots take the seats nobody has joined.}
\end{keytable}

\subsection{Your turn}
\begin{keytable}
\kr{\key{LEFT}\ \ \key{RIGHT}}{\VC{spike}: move the cursor to an \V{exploit} in your \V{kit}; a box shows its \V{node}. An inverse one can't run now; pick it and the prompt line says why.}
\kr{\key{A}}{Run it.}
\kr{\key{LEFT}\ \ \key{RIGHT}\ \ \key{A}}{\VC{infiltration}: the syndicate to \V{broker} the new \V{hive} to. A tied \V{hivejack}: the \V{hive} that survives, or is \V{absorbed} next.}
\kr{\key{UP}\ \ \key{DOWN}}{Buy \V{coin}: the arrow in the \Vp{hive} panel, on the syndicates with a \V{hive}.}
\kr{\key{A}}{Buy one \V{coin} of it, up to \Rule{maxbuy} in any mix.}
\kr{\key{B}}{Take back the last \V{coin} in a buy.}
\kr{\key{START}}{Confirm the buy. \key{START} alone buys nothing.}
\kr{\key{A}\ \ \key{B}}{When the game may end: \V{nullroute} the region now, or play on. \key{START} plays on too.}
\end{keytable}

\subsection{When your \V{hive} is \V{absorbed}}
\begin{keytable}
\kr{\key{UP}\ \ \key{DOWN}\ \ \key{A}}{First, how many \Vp{coin} to \V{cashout}; then how many to \V{swap}, two at a time.
The rest you \V{hodl}. The count goes no higher than you can: what you hold, and twice what the \V{bank} has left.}
\end{keytable}

\subsection{Leaving}
\begin{keytable}
\kr{\key{SELECT}+\key{START}\ \ \key{A}}{\VC{logoff} for good. A bot takes over your seat at once, and you're back on the title screen.
\key{B} or \key{START} stays.}
\end{keytable}

\chapter{Frequently Asked Questions}
\begin{multicols}{2}
\rules{faq}

\subsection{On the NES}
\begin{description}[leftmargin=0pt,labelsep=0.5em]
  \item[Which machines?] An NES with a FujiNet NES cartridge, and any TV. NTSC and PAL both:
    the shot clock counts 60 or 50 frames a second, whichever the console runs.
  \item[Do I need a keyboard?] No. Everything is on the pad in port 1.
  \item[Can I play alone?] Yes. Host a game and start it with \Rule{minplayers} seats: one bot plays you. Any seat
    nobody takes goes to a bot.
  \item[What are the digits on the \V{grid}?] Move hints: each marks a \V{node} that an \V{exploit} in your \V{kit}
    can run on, and the digit is its place in the \V{kit}. \key{SELECT}+\key{A} turns them on or off.
  \item[What is the yellow box on the \V{grid}?] The \V{node} the \V{exploit} under the cursor would \V{spike}.
  \item[Why are some numbers yellow?] Cash, and every other syndicate's \V{coin}, so two numbers side by side
    read apart: \code{3} next to \code{12} is not \code{312}.
  \item[What do \code{*} and \code{?} before a name mean?] \code{*} is a bot. \code{?} is a \V{player} who is away,
    or on autopilot; a bot plays for them meanwhile.
  \item[Can I turn the sound off?] Yes: \key{SELECT}+\key{B}, any time. A chord says it's back on.
  \item[Can I play against an Atari, an Apple II, a PC or a CoCo?] Yes. The servers are the same for every
    machine: all of them meet at the same tables.
  \item[Why was my handle refused?] Someone at the table has it, a bot included. If it's your own seat after a
    reset, the server may not have noticed the drop yet: try again in a minute.
  \item[What if everybody leaves?] The game waits for 10 minutes with nobody connected, then it is dropped.
\end{description}
\end{multicols}
