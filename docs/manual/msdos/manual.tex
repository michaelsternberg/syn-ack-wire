\chapter{\VC{jackin}}
\game\ runs from DOS, with an RS-232 FujiNet on a serial port. First load FujiNet's driver, one of
the FujiNet RS-232 tools, with \code{DEVICE=FUJINET.SYS} in CONFIG.SYS. Then pick a \game\ server in the FujiNet lobby,
which mounts the disk and starts the game, or start it yourself:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
  \item Put the \game\ disk in a FujiNet slot: on FujiNet's web page, or with \code{FCONFIG}.
  \item Mount it with \code{FMOUNT -A}, change to its drive, and type \code{SYNACK}.
\end{enumerate}
From the lobby:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item The server you pick reaches the game through FujiNet's SD card. Without one, the game dials its own
    server, CHIBA-GRID-7, whichever you picked: put any SD card in the FujiNet to join the others.
  \item To start the game, the lobby looks for \code{COMMAND.COM} on the path: if the screen stays blank,
    put \code{PATH C:\textbackslash} in AUTOEXEC.BAT.
\end{itemize}
\begin{sloppypar}
On a colour display the game first asks for the screen width: \key{1} for 40 columns, clearer on a TV or a
composite monitor, or \key{2} for 80. To skip the question, add \code{SET~SYNACK=40} or \code{SET~SYNACK=80} to
AUTOEXEC.BAT. On the title screen, \key{F4} swaps them.
\end{sloppypar}

The title screen dials the server as soon as it appears. Type a handle, up to 8 characters, and press
\key{Enter}.

\chapter{The Screen}
\begin{sloppypar}
Everything you need is on one screen: its middle 40 columns in 80, with a circuit board at the sides, or all
of it in 40. It starts out alike on any display, from a monochrome card to VGA or a laptop's grey LCD. On a
colour display, the \key{F5} key turns on green phosphor, with each syndicate in its own colour and your
questions on magenta. This is early in a game, and
it's your turn: pick an \V{exploit} to run.
\end{sloppypar}

\renewcommand{\secstatus}{THE KEYBOARD \quad // \quad FUNCTION KEYS \quad // \quad NO MOUSE}
\chapter{Keys}
\game\ is played from the keyboard. Each question on the prompt line names the keys it takes; this chapter
has them all in one place. The game checks an answer before sending it and says on the prompt line what's
wrong, such as \code{NOT ENOUGH NEW YEN}.

\subsection{Any time}
\begin{keytable}
\kr{\key{F1}\ \ \key{?}}{Open the instructions, on the title screen or in the middle of a game.}
\kr{\key{F2}}{Move hints on or off: the digits on the \V{grid} that show where each \V{exploit} in your \V{kit} can run.}
\kr{\key{F3}}{Sound on or off. A chord tells you it's on.}
\kr{\key{F5}}{Colour on or off, on a colour display.}
\end{keytable}

\subsection{The title screen}
\begin{keytable}
\kr{\key{A}--\key{Z}\ \ \key{0}--\key{9}}{Type your handle, up to 8 characters.}
\kr{\key{Backspace}}{Rub out the last character.}
\kr{\key{Enter}}{\VC{jackin}.}
\kr{\key{F4}}{40 or 80 columns (a PCjr: \key{Fn} and \key{4}).}
\end{keytable}

\subsection{The instructions}
\begin{keytable}
\kr{\key{>}\ \ \key{\fontsize{12}{9}\selectfont$\rightarrow$}}{Next page.}
\kr{\key{<}\ \ \key{\fontsize{12}{9}\selectfont$\leftarrow$}}{Previous page.}
\kr{\key{Esc}}{Back to where you were. \key{Enter}, \key{F1} and \key{?} do the same.}
\end{keytable}

\subsection{Hosting}
\begin{keytable}
\kr{\key{2}--\key{6}}{Start the game with that many seats. Bots take the seats nobody has joined.}
\end{keytable}

\subsection{Your turn}
\begin{keytable}
\kr{\key{1}--\key{6}}{\VC{spike}: run that \V{exploit} from your \V{kit}. A reverse one can't run now; press it and the prompt line says why.}
\kr{\key{A}--\key{Z}}{\VC{infiltration}: the letter of the syndicate to \V{broker} the new \V{hive} to.}
\kr{\key{A}--\key{Z}}{A tied \V{hivejack}: the letter of the \V{hive} that survives, or is \V{absorbed} next.}
\kr{\key{A}--\key{Z}}{Buy \V{coin}: type the syndicate letters, up to \Rule{maxbuy}, in any mix of the \Vp{hive} on the \V{grid}.
One letter per \V{coin}: \key{\Syn{5}{letter}} buys one, \key{\Syn{5}{letter}}\key{\Syn{5}{letter}} two, \key{\Syn{5}{letter}}\key{\Syn{5}{letter}}\key{\Syn{5}{letter}} three of the same, \key{\Syn{5}{letter}}\key{\Syn{0}{letter}} one each of two, \key{\Syn{5}{letter}}\key{\Syn{0}{letter}}\key{\Syn{6}{letter}} one each of three.}
\kr{\key{Backspace}}{Take back the last \V{coin} in a buy. \key{Delete} does the same.}
\kr{\key{Enter}}{Confirm the buy. \key{Enter} alone buys nothing.}
\kr{\key{Y}\ \ \key{N}}{When the game may end: \V{nullroute} the region now, or play on. \key{Enter} plays on too.}
\end{keytable}

\subsection{When your \V{hive} is \V{absorbed}}
\begin{keytable}
\kr{\key{0}--\key{9}\ \ \key{Enter}}{First, how many \Vp{coin} to \V{cashout}; then how many to \V{swap}, an even number.
The rest you \V{hodl}. \key{Backspace} rubs out a digit.}
\end{keytable}

\subsection{Leaving}
\begin{keytable}
\kr{\key{Esc}\ \ \key{Y}}{\VC{logoff} for good. A bot takes over your seat at once, and you're back on the title screen.
Any key but \key{Y} stays.}
\end{keytable}

\chapter{Frequently Asked Questions}
\begin{multicols}{2}
\rules{faq}

\subsection{On the PC}
\begin{description}[leftmargin=0pt,labelsep=0.5em]
  \item[Which PCs?] Any IBM PC or compatible from the first 4.77 MHz PC on, DOS 2 or later, with an RS-232 FujiNet
    and its \code{FUJINET.SYS} loaded. The game is all text, so it looks alike on any display: MDA, Hercules, CGA,
    EGA, VGA or a laptop's LCD.
  \item[What do I need in CONFIG.SYS?] \code{DEVICE=FUJINET.SYS}, from the same FujiNet firmware version as your
    FujiNet. It uses \code{COM1} at 115200 bps unless you set \code{FUJI\_PORT} and \code{FUJI\_BPS}.
  \item[Can I play alone?] Yes. Host a game and press \key{\Rule{minplayers}}: one bot plays you. Any seat nobody
    takes goes to a bot.
  \item[What are the digits on the \V{grid}?] Move hints: each marks a \V{node} that an \V{exploit} in your \V{kit}
    can run on, and the digit is its key. \key{F2} turns them on or off.
  \item[What do \code{*} and \code{?} after a name mean?] \code{*} is a bot. \code{?} is a \V{player} who is away,
    or on autopilot; a bot plays for them meanwhile.
  \item[Can I turn the sound off?] Yes: \key{F3}, any time. A chord says it's back on.
  \item[What do I hear?] With an AdLib or a Sound Blaster, the chords play on its music chip; otherwise on the
    PC's speaker, a note at a time.
  \item[What is the art at the sides?] Decoration: a circuit board, the same all game. It shows nothing the
    middle 40 columns don't.
  \item[Can I play against an Atari or an Apple II?] Yes. The servers are the same for every machine: PC, Apple II
    and Atari players meet at the same tables.
  \item[Why was my handle refused?] Someone at the table has it, a bot included. If it's your own seat after a
    reboot, the server may not have noticed the drop yet: try again in a minute.
  \item[What if everybody leaves?] The game waits for 10 minutes with nobody connected, then it is dropped.
\end{description}
\end{multicols}
